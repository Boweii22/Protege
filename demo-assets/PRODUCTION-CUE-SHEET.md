# Protégé — Final 2-Minute Voiceover, Transition and Sound Cue Sheet

## How to use this document

Record the voiceover as **eleven separate clips**, one clip for each numbered scene below. Do not generate one continuous two-minute ElevenLabs file. Separate clips let you place every sentence precisely, regenerate weak performances, and preserve intentional silence.

The timeline assumes 30fps. Times ending in `.0`, `.2`, or `.5` do not need frame-perfect precision; allow roughly three frames of tolerance. Never time-stretch the voice by more than 3%. If a line runs long, regenerate it slightly faster rather than cutting breaths mid-word.

Use one music track for the whole film. The sound design should feel tactile—paper, pencil, restrained mechanical movement—not futuristic bleeps.

## Audio hierarchy

1. Voiceover: target approximately **−6 dB peak**, clear and central.
2. Music under speech: approximately **−25 to −21 dB**.
3. Sound effects under speech: approximately **−24 to −18 dB**.
4. Hero effects in deliberate gaps: up to approximately **−13 dB**.
5. Master loudness: approximately **−14 LUFS integrated**, peak below **−1 dBTP**.

Apply a short 3–5 frame audio fade to every voice clip. Duck the music by 3–5 dB whenever narration begins. Use no reverb on the narrator.

# Exact transition inventory

Use only these transitions throughout the finished film:

1. **Hard interface cut:** user actions, sends, selections and evidence changes.
2. **Physical ivory paper wipe:** opening into product, technical montage into closing.
3. **Match cut:** red misconception before/after explanation; score evidence into Learning Map.
4. **Gentle push:** headline, mental model and diagnostic evidence.
5. **Short match dissolve:** only between closely related landing-page compositions.

Never use glitches, whip pans, spin transitions, fake camera shake, lens flares, chromatic aberration or generic zoom presets.

# Sound-effect shopping list

Download clean, isolated, licensed versions of:

- 3–4 short graphite writing scratches
- 1 pencil underline
- 1 pencil circle
- 1 pencil cross/incorrect mark
- 2 soft paper sheet slides
- 2 paper flick/message-send sounds
- 3 muted mouse/interface clicks
- 4–6 tiny wood or paper ticks
- 1 paper envelope seal
- 1 rubber library stamp
- 2 restrained low impacts: one medium, one very soft
- 1 short bright tonal shimmer
- 1 subtle reverse paper rub/riser

Avoid keyboard, click and impact sounds that are recognisable from common editing templates. Pitch-shift repeated ticks by ±1–2 semitones and vary their volume so the soundscape does not feel copied and pasted.

# Final mix check

- Watch once with headphones.
- Watch once through laptop speakers at 40% volume.
- Watch once muted to confirm captions and visual logic still communicate the product.
- If a sound effect attracts attention away from a word, lower it by 3 dB.
- If music makes the narration feel rushed, lower the music rather than speeding the voice.
- Preserve the deliberate silence before the score. It is one of the most effective sound cues in the entire film.
